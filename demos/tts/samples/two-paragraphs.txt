Philadelphia sits where the Delaware meets the Schuylkill, and the grid William Penn drew in sixteen eighty two still holds the centre of the city together today. Market Street runs east to west. Broad Street runs north to south. The numbers climb as you walk away from the river, which means a stranger with no map can still work out roughly where they are. That habit of legible design is a good thing to keep in mind when you build something for other people to use in a hurry. A person at a hackathon table has about ten seconds of patience, so the first thing they touch has to do something they can hear.

This demo reads text aloud with an open model instead of a closed one. The text is split into short chunks at paragraph and sentence boundaries, each chunk is sent to RunPod in order, and every finished chunk is streamed straight back to the browser. That is why the first sentence starts playing while the last sentence is still being generated somewhere on a GPU. The chunks are scheduled back to back in the browser with the Web Audio clock, so there is no gap at the seams. At the end the server joins every chunk into one wav file, keeps it on disk, and hands back a link you can download. Nothing depends on the temporary audio links RunPod returns, because those expire after seven days and a demo people bookmark has to outlive that.
